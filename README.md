# Casian Fitness Log

Weekly training plan arranged as alternating pairs, personal records, meal tracking with nutrition label scanning, a table of c.1,000 foods without a label, cooked weights and own recipes, weekly progress photos and a weight log, and the training data of a Garmin watch through intervals.icu, synced on Wi-Fi only. Installs to the home screen, works offline and keeps all data on the phone.

## Put it online (one time, free)

1. Create a free account on github.com
2. Create a new repository called `fitness-log` and set it to Public
3. On the repository page, tap Add file, then Upload files, select all the files from this folder and tap Commit changes
4. Open Settings, then Pages. Under Source choose Deploy from a branch, pick `main` and `/ (root)`, then Save
5. After a minute or two the address appears at the top of the Pages screen, for example `https://your-name.github.io/fitness-log/`

## Install on the phone

- iPhone: open the address in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen
- Android: open the address in Chrome, tap the menu, then Install app

Then open the app from its new icon and tap Load backup file to bring in the plan and records.

## Label scanning

- In an ingredient, tap Scan label: the phone's own camera app opens. Take the photo there; back in the app it shows full screen with Try again and OK at the bottom. After OK, drag the corners of the box so that it holds the nutrition table and tap Read this area. The box need not be exact: the reader finds the table inside it and reads that part again on its own. Drawing the box is still worth it: a whole photo reads less well, most of all on round tubs and cans
- Gallery, next to Scan label, takes a photo you already have straight to the crop box. If the phone closes the browser while its camera app is open (it can, when short of memory), the ingredient you were filling in comes back when the app opens again; take the photo again then
- Scanning again in the same ingredient clears what the earlier scan filled in and keeps what you typed by hand
- The reader finds every piece of text in the crop with one small neural network, reads each piece with two different ones and puts names and values into rows and columns. A value on which the two readings agree is filled in and marked Scan. A value on which they differ, or that only one of them found, is marked Check (unless the calories, protein, carbs and fat add up with it), and the note under the scan gives both readings; check those against the label first
- The first scan downloads the reader's model files from this site (c.17 MB, or c.6 MB for the fast reader) and its runtime from a public CDN (c.11 MB), and needs an internet connection; after that scanning works offline. The photo never leaves the phone
- A photo taken sideways or upside down is turned automatically (a sideways photo the right way round at the first try); Rotate in the crop view turns it by hand
- A vitamins table under the main table is read as a table of its own, so both can be in one box; if a vitamin is missed, scan that table alone
- Romanian, English and the other languages found on Lidl labels are supported, with the app staying in English
- Settings has the choice of reader: New reader, accurate (the default, two readings), New reader, fast (one reading with smaller models, for a slow phone) and Classic reader (the one of 1.2.9, which also takes over by itself when the new reader cannot start)
- Scan reports: every scan keeps the picture the reader was given, what it read, how long it took and the values saved afterwards, for the last 12 scans, on the phone only. When scans go wrong, tap Save report file in Settings and send the file to the developer, who can replay exactly those scans; nothing is sent by the app itself

## Market: foods without a label

- The Food tab has a third view, Market: 1,035 everyday foods bought without a label. 904 come from the French national food table (ANSES Ciqual 2025, values per 100 g): fruit, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, potatoes, meat by cut (raw and cooked), cold cuts and sausages, fish and seafood, eggs, cheese, bread and pastries, cooked rice, pasta and grains, oils and fats, herbs, spices and salt, sugar and honey. The other 131 are the products of the Lidl bakery, from Lidl Romania's own sheet
- Search in English or Romanian (mere, piept pui, salau). A few Romanian products are shown as similar to the closest food in the table (telemea, cascaval, branza de vaci, urda, slanina, kaizer, mamaliga, carne tocata de porc); their own values may differ
- Tap a food for all its values with vitamins and minerals, a typical piece weight where it helps (1 egg c.50 g) and, for raw meat and fish, typical cooked values
- In a meal, Add ingredient finds the Market foods below your own ingredients, marked Market. Copy to Ingredients makes a copy you can change
- Where the table gives a value as below a limit ("< 0,5") it counts as half of it, "traces" as 0, and a value the table does not give stays empty
- The table is the file foods-v1.json, uploaded once next to index.html; the app keeps it on the phone for offline use

## Cooked weights

- For food weighed raw and eaten cooked (pasta, rice, legumes, meat): in Add ingredient tap Cooked, weigh the batch raw and then cooked once, and log the weight on the plate. The values are the raw (label) values divided by the ratio (100 g of pasta giving 250 g cooked: x2.50), because cooking adds water but no nutrients
- The batch is saved with the ingredient (or with the Market food), so next time only the plate is weighed; New batch replaces it. The ingredient's form shows the batch weights too
- Meat and fish not weighed raw: tap how it was cooked (pan or grill, oven, boiled or steamed, stewed or braised) for a typical ratio of that kind of meat or fish, marked approximate; a batch you weigh replaces it. Your own meat ingredients take their kind in the form or in Add ingredient. A typical ratio can be off by 10% or more
- Salt added to the cooking water is not in the values

## Recipes

- The Food tab's fourth view, Recipes, makes a food of your own from your ingredients and the Market foods with their weights: a cake, a stew, a sauce
- Weigh the dish when it is done (a cake loses water in the oven, rice takes some up) and enter that weight: the values per 100 g are those of the whole dish divided by it. Without it the app divides by the sum of the ingredients' weights
- Pieces (8 slices) give the weight and the values of one piece
- In a meal, Add ingredient finds your recipes, marked Recipe; Use 1 piece fills in the weight of one piece. A recipe changed later leaves the meals already logged as they were, and keeps its values when one of its own ingredients is deleted
- Recipes are in the backup file

## Body: progress photos (beta) and weight

- The Body tab has three views: Photos, Weight and Info
- Photos holds weekly albums of progress photos. Each week has a slot for each of six poses (front relaxed, front flexed, left side, right side, back relaxed, back flexed) and Unorganized for any other photo of that week. Week 1 is the first week with photos
- Tap a pose to take its photo with the phone's camera app or to pick one from the gallery (Unorganized takes several at once). A pose holds one photo a week: a new one moves the earlier one to Unorganized, so nothing is lost by mistake
- Tap a photo to see it full screen: the arrows step through the weeks of that pose, Compare puts two weeks side by side, Move puts it in another pose (two photos swap places), Replace takes it again and Delete removes it. Poses shows every week of one pose, with Compare first and latest
- Compare, next to Weeks and Poses, shows one pose in two weeks (the first and the latest to start with; Before and After choose others), side by side or one over the other with a slider; Full screen opens the two at full size
- Weight logs your weight with the date: weigh in the morning, after the toilet, before eating or drinking, on the same scale. It shows the latest weight, the average of the last 7 days, the change against the week before, the change per week over 4 weeks and since the first weigh-in, with a chart of the last 12 weeks (each weigh-in and the 7-day average). Tap a weigh-in to change its date or weight, or to delete it. The weigh-ins are in the backup file
- Info has How to take them (once a week on the same day, in the morning before eating, with the same place, light, clothes and distance, the camera at chest height and the whole body in the frame) and Keep a copy
- The photos stay on the phone and are not in the backup file. Save all photos, in Info, makes a zip (one folder a week, plus photos.json); Load photos brings a zip back, on the same phone or a new one. Keep the zip in Google Drive or on a computer, never in the repository
- Beta: the layout may still change. Photos are kept at up to 1920 pixels on the long side (c.0.4 MB each)

## Garmin (through intervals.icu)

- The Garmin tab shows what the watch recorded: last night's sleep against the 8-hour target (with the plan's rule: under 8 hours, a shorter morning run or none), a recovery check (resting heart rate and HRV against the last 7 days), this week's sessions against the plan, the training load, running (km per week, the longest run, time in heart-rate zones), treadmill heart rate at speed and the strength sessions with their effort rating. No records, VO2max or weight
- The data comes through intervals.icu, a free training site linked to Garmin Connect: watch, Garmin Connect, intervals.icu, the app. Garmin's own API refuses calls from web apps like this one (found with 1.3.1 on 22-Nov-26)
- Setting up: in intervals.icu, Settings, Garmin Connect, connect Garmin and tick Download activities and Download wellness data (sleep, HRV, resting HR); Download old data brings the last weeks. Then, in Settings, Developer Settings, generate an API key and paste it in the app's Settings, Garmin (Open intervals.icu settings goes there). Test connection says what the last 7 days hold: the activities, and the nights with sleep, HRV and resting heart rate
- If sleep, HRV or resting heart rate stay missing: check Download wellness data and its fields in intervals.icu, check in the Garmin Connect app (connected apps) that intervals.icu may read health data, or disconnect Garmin in intervals.icu and connect it again. While they are missing, the tab says so and the recovery check waits for them
- Training load: each session counts its effort rating times its minutes; a session without a rating is estimated from its heart-rate zones. The last 7 days are set against the average week of the last 4: well above it (more than x1.3) is a big jump, a time to keep the next days easy
- Zones: intervals.icu's own heart-rate zones (set them to match Garmin's in intervals.icu's settings for running); with more than five, the zones above zone 4 count as zone 5. The easy runs (Tuesday and Sunday) should stay mostly in zones 1 and 2, and the HIIT on Wednesday should reach zones 4 and 5. Treadmill: the same speed at a lower heart rate means you are fitter; the tab sets the metres per heartbeat of the latest runs against those of three weeks before
- Effort rating: rate each session on the watch when it asks (Self Evaluation, on for strength as for runs); a rating added later in the Garmin Connect app does not reach intervals.icu. HRV: Garmin's HRV status does not pass through intervals.icu, so the check sets last night's HRV against the 7-day average
- Treadmill speed: the watch over-reads on the treadmill unless the run is calibrated: at the end of each treadmill run choose Calibrate & Save and enter the treadmill's distance. Settings, Garmin, Treadmill speed can instead correct the watch's speed with your figures (5:30 per km shown at 10 km/h, 5:15 at 11, 5:00 at 12), approximate
- Syncing: only while the phone is on Wi-Fi (as Chrome reports it): when the app opens or comes back to the screen and when the phone joins Wi-Fi (30 minutes at least between these), and with Sync now. Each sync reads the last 8 weeks again, so whatever reaches intervals.icu late (older days it brings from Garmin, a late watch sync) shows at the next sync. Off Wi-Fi the app stays offline and shows the last synced data
- The key stays on the phone: it is not in the backup file, and it must never go into the repository or a message. It does not run out; if someone else may have seen it, generate a new one in intervals.icu (the old one stops working) and paste it in Settings. Remove key takes it off the phone. The Garmin key of 1.3.1 is taken off the phone by itself
- Save sync report (Settings) keeps intervals.icu's answers to the last sync, cut short and without the key, to send to the developer when something in the tab looks wrong
- The Garmin data stays on the phone in a database of its own and is not in the backup file (on a new phone it syncs again)

## Notes

- The code in the repository is public; the log itself never leaves the phone. Never upload a backup file, a photos zip or a sync report to the repository, and never put the intervals.icu key in a file there
- On iPhone, the home-screen app and Safari keep separate data, so load the backup from inside the home-screen app
- Save a backup from Settings every week or two and keep it in Files, iCloud Drive or Google Drive
- To update the app, upload the new files over the old ones; the app shows "A new version is ready" on its next launch
- The reader's model files (ocr-det-v5.onnx, ocr-rec-latin-v5.onnx, ocr-det-tiny.onnx, ocr-rec-tiny.onnx) are uploaded once, next to index.html; later updates need only index.html, sw.js and README.md. A changed model would come under a new file name. The food table (foods-v1.json) is uploaded once too, with 1.3.0; a changed table would come as foods-v2.json

- 1.6.1 (Nov-26): a decimal comma printed tight against a digit, so that the two become one glyph (53,3 read as 33, 24,5 as 45, 12,1 as 1 on a Lidl almond bag), is now recognised: the digit recogniser has classes for a digit fused with its comma on either side, and a fused glyph that hangs below the baseline in front of another digit counts as a digit, not as a unit; the value strip starts 1.5 character heights left of the leftmost number the first read saw, so a value whose first digit the first read dropped (454 for 25,4) is no longer cut off; the digit recogniser measures contrast on each row's own glyphs instead of the whole strip; a name line that wraps under its value (od kojih secera / din care zaharuri) is attached by the typical extent of its words, so one stray tall word no longer pulls it onto the row above; a whole number of 1000 or more is taken as kJ, never as grams.
